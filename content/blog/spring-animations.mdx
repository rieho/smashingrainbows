Spring animations create motion that feels organic. Instead of easing curves with fixed durations, springs simulate physical forces — mass, tension, and friction — producing movement that responds naturally to interruption.

## Why springs over easing

Traditional CSS easing (`ease-in-out`, cubic bezier) has a fixed duration. If you interrupt the animation mid-way, it snaps or awkwardly restarts. Springs don't have a fixed duration — they settle when the physics say they should.

This makes springs ideal for:

- **Drag and release** — the element springs back naturally
- **Interrupted animations** — redirecting motion feels smooth
- **Responsive feedback** — different spring configs convey different "weights"

## Parameters to experiment with

| Parameter | Effect | Range |
|-----------|--------|-------|
| `stiffness` | How "snappy" the spring is | 50–500 |
| `damping` | How quickly it settles | 10–40 |
| `mass` | Inertia of the element | 0.5–5 |

Higher stiffness = faster, bouncier. Higher damping = more controlled, less overshoot.
